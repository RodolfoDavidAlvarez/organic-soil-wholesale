by Soil Seed & Water Group · coordination checklist
Operating Notes to Remember
Light-product conversionFor potting soil and mulch, use 24 tons = approximately 60 cu yd. That means 1 ton is about 2.5 cu yd, and 1 cu yd is about 0.4 ton.
Density changesMoisture, matting, compression, cubic yards, and weight are estimates. They are not perfect constants.
Heavy productsDairy compost, worm castings, sand, and wetter products may be handled more by tonnage than cubic yards.

Pickup Locations

Local pickup requires coordination and loader availability before confirming the customer order.

Phoenix yard: 1634 N 19th Ave, Phoenix, AZ 85009
Congress plant: 18980 Stanton Rd, Congress, AZ 85332

Loader and Scale Notes

  • Congress plant has a scale. Weigh empty, weigh full, then calculate net weight.
  • Phoenix does not have a scale. Estimate by bucket size.
  • Confirm whether the Bobcat skid steer loader or green loader is available.
  • Confirm bucket size before quoting final pickup volume.

Tote / Super Sack Checks

  • Ask whether the customer can unload the tote.
  • Forklift is best. Manual unloading is possible but not recommended.
  • Store covered and use reasonably soon because the sack breathes and moisture can drop within weeks.
  • Use totes when the customer needs better pricing but still needs movable product.

Truckload Access Checks

  • Confirm safe access, turning room, and unloading space.
  • Flatbed loads are best for pallets, totes, mixed loads, and Moffett unloading.
  • Walking floor loads are best when the customer can receive a large loose pile.
  • For loose loads, confirm room for truck access, turning, dumping, and product storage.

Before Confirming

  • Check product availability.
  • Check loader availability.
  • Clarify cubic-yard pickup vs full truckload pricing.
  • Confirm access, unloading equipment, and whether the customer can receive loose material.

Best-Fit Guidance

  • Bags: easiest to store, count, and resell.
  • Pallets: best full-bag format for retailers, contractors, nurseries, and one-time jobs.
  • Totes: better price and still movable, but customer needs unloading equipment.
  • Loose truckload: best for large use-now volume.
